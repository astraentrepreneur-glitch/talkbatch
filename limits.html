Original compressed quality or codec preservation

This preview re-encodes video to VP9 and audio to Opus. It does not preserve the source bitstream. Quality and file size can change; successful export is not proof that the result looks or sounds acceptable.

MP4 delivery

Outputs are WebM, not MP4. Do not treat the ability to read an MP4 as a promise to preserve its container or codecs.

Multiple audio tracks, subtitles, chapters or source metadata

The exporter uses the first video and first audio streams. Extra tracks, subtitles, chapters and source metadata are dropped. A source whose first video stream is attached cover art is rejected.

A browser-only export or a native editor integration

The browser previews a local file and downloads a JSON plan. Exporting still requires local Python, FFmpeg and ffprobe. The PyPI wheel contains only the exporter; no browser planner or media tools.

A long-recording speed or synchronization guarantee

The queue is sequential and may decode substantial input again for each output. The small synthetic example establishes neither production throughput nor perfect speech synchronization on your recording.

Why not promise both exact cuts and stream-copy?

Inter-frame codecs can make a requested boundary depend on earlier encoded frames. Copying compressed packets and selecting only the intended decoded frames are different operations.

An archived pre-product synthetic experiment informed this choice: a particular FFmpeg seek/copy, timestamp-normalization and concat method retained unwanted leading frames at off-keyframe boundaries. That was not a test of LosslessCut or a claim that every stream-copy or smart-cut workflow behaves that way. The closed experiment is not a new product benchmark.

TalkBatch instead trims decoded video/audio, resets timestamps, joins the selected ranges and re-encodes them. Cuts are still constrained by actual frame boundaries. This does not promise sub-frame precision, variable-frame-rate equivalence or unchanged image/audio quality.

What the current example actually establishes

The reproducible example uses a twelve-second synthetic source with frame-coded stripes and a tone, not customer footage. Its one-, two- and three-range outputs have expected frame sequences that can be decoded and compared.

The current source tests also cover a silent/reordered case and a small MPEG-4 Part 2/AAC MP4 input. They do not establish every codec, platform, long recording or real conference job. Audio-stream presence is not a listening or lip-sync test.

The exporter's codec/stream/duration checks are sanity checks. A completed report.json is not proof of correct speech, acceptable quality or customer acceptance.

A safer first use

  1. Decide whether WebM output and the track/quality tradeoffs fit the job. If not, choose an export path that supports your actual requirements.
  2. Try one short range and one joined group from your own non-sensitive sample. Use a new output directory; previous outputs are not overwritten.
  3. Watch and listen to the results, especially around joins. Compare against the source before committing a long queue.
  4. If reporting a problem, give versions, intended ranges and a small reproducible synthetic example where possible. Do not upload private recordings, personal paths, payment details or an unreviewed report.

--dry-run validates the plan and probes the source; it does not encode anything or replace reviewing an actual sample export.
